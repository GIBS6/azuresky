Sampling Procedure for Coal(In Accordance with ISO 18283:2006)
1. Purpose
This procedure describes how representative samples of coal are to be obtained, reduced, and preserved for the preparation of reference materials (RMs/CRMs) or proficiency testing (PT) items. The procedure ensures that coal samples are collected in line with ISO 18283 and meet the requirements of ISO 17034 and ISO Guide 35.
2. Scope
This procedure applies to all coal consignments (e.g., bituminous, sub-bituminous, anthracite, lignite) sampled by the RMP for CRM production or PT schemes. It covers primary sampling, gross sample formation, sub-sampling, and reduction up to the stage where test or reference portions are obtained.
3. References
ISO 18283:2006 – Hard coal and coke — Manual sampling
ISO Guide 35 – Reference materials — General and statistical principles
ISO 17034 – General requirements for the competence of reference material producers
ISO/IEC 17043 – Proficiency testing — General requirements
4. Responsibilities
Sampler: Executes the sampling strictly according to this SOP.
Operations Manager and/or Laboratory Supervisor: Ensures the adequacy of sampling plans and equipment.
Laboratory Manager: Confirms that sampling records and chain custody are complete and traceable.
5. Definitions
Increment: A quantity of coal collected at one point from the lot.
Gross Sample: The aggregate of all increments from the lot.
Sub-sample: A reduced, representative portion of the gross sample.
Test/Reference Portion: The final portion prepared for analysis or CRM production.
6. Equipment
Manual shovel or scoop (flat-edged, clean, non-contaminating material)
Sample containers: moisture-tight, dustproof, clearly labelable
Riffle splitter or rotary divider for sample reduction (cone and quartering)
Calibrated balance
Moisture-proof storage bags 
7. Procedure
7.1 Sampling Plan
Define lot size (e.g., 1,000 tons of coal).
Determine number of increments according to ISO 18283:
Minimum of 35 increments for lots >100 tons.
For smaller lots, increments should be sufficient to cover the full particle size distribution.
Document plan in the Sampling Record Form.7.2 Collection of Increments-Take increments from the moving stream (preferred) or a static pile (if unavoidable).- Each increment shall be at least 3 times the nominal top size (NTS) of the coal particles.- Collect increments at evenly distributed time/space intervals to ensure representativity.- Avoid bias (e.g., do not select only fine or coarse material).7.3 Formation of Gross Sample- Combine all increments into a single gross sample.- Mix thoroughly to minimize segregation.- Weigh and record the total mass of the gross sample.7.4 Sample Reduction- Reduce the gross sample to a manageable size using riffle splitters or rotary dividers (never by coning and quartering unless validated).- Continue reduction until the sub-sample is:  * Large enough for homogeneity and stability testing  * Suitable for CRM production or PT distribution- Record reduction steps, including masses before and after each reduction.7.5 Sample Handling & Preservation- Transfer sub-samples into clean, moisture-tight containers.- Label each container with:  * Sample ID  * Date- Store samples in a cool, dry environment to prevent moisture loss and oxidation.
8. Quality Control
- Collect duplicate gross samples for reproducibility checks.- Retain a reference sample (archive) for traceability.- Record ambient conditions (temperature, humidity) at the time of sampling.- Verify homogeneity of reduced samples by testing subsamples.
9. Safety
- Wear PPE (dust mask/respirator, gloves, goggles, safety boots).- Minimize exposure to coal dust; use dust suppression where possible.- Follow site safety rules for working near stockpiles and conveyors.
10. Records
- Sampling Record Form (lot details, increment plan, sampler signature).- Gross and reduced sample weights.- Chain-of-custody forms.- QC check records.- Equipment calibration logs.
