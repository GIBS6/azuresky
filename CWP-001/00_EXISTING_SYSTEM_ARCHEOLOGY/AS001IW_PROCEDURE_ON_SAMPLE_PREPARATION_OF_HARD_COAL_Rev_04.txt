INTRODUCTION
This procedure specifies the requirements for the coal sample preparation procedure of proficiency testing scheme and certified reference material. A single sample is obtained from a coal mine or coal laboratory. Constituents of a sample are increments taken from a lot/stockpile.
SCOPE
This procedure describes the preparation of samples of coal from a combination of increments representative of the lot.
Normative reference
ISO 13909-4 Hard coal and coke – Mechanical sampling Part 4: Coal preparation of test samples.

General analysis
note: The 25mm and 10mm crusher efficiencies are to be done every Monday, at the beginning of the shift (before any work commences). the 3mm and 4.75mm efficiency will be done on the day it is required for use (before using it).
Request coal sample ±200 kg from a mine or coal laboratory.
Record the details of the sample onto the receiving book.
Weigh and Air dry the sample until it is visibly dry.
Cone and Quarter the sample, combine two opposites to continue the process with, and package the two remaining opposites.
Rationale: While cone and quartering has its limitations in terms of homogeneity across the quarters, the core principle is not for the chosen opposites and the packaged sample to have any relationship, but it is for the operators to have a workable sample to continue the process with, since working with the entire sample would be impractical given that we do not reduce at each crushing stage. Through the homogeneity studies conducted, the homogeneity drum proves to be effective in homogenizing the entire sample, thereby eliminating any doubt that may arise from cone and quartering. 
A sample will only be considered a secondary batch to an already established CRM after it has independently gone through PT and its statistical analysis concludes that each parameter is reproducible to an established CRM. This eliminates any link that may arise on the packaged samples with the initially processed sample.
If there are multiple samples of unknown grades, start by collecting small portions (<5kg) from each of the individual samples for ash indicative analysis. Stage crush to 3mm, pulverize, and take to the lab. Upon receiving results, the samples shall be grouped according to the following ranges: 
10-15 (%) ash
16-20 (%) ash
21-24 (%) ash
25-28 (%) ash
29-32 (%) ash
33-36 (%) ash
37-40 (%) ash
NB: Discard any samples with an ash >40%.
For samples that come from one stockpile, there is no need to perform an indicative analysis, proceed to crushing after drying.
Crush the entire sample in the 25mm crusher.
Crush the entire sample in the 10mm crusher.
Next, crush the entire sample in the 3mm crusher.
Air-dry the sample in an oven ≤ 40 °C for ≤4 hours.
Pulverise the entire sample.
Transfer the sample into the 210L drum, strap the drum onto the mixer and homogenise for 4 hours.
Finally, take increments of ±600g in a pan. Use the flat heap method to take portions from 600g and transfer into ~100g bottles for packaging.
In the process take 10 random samples which will be used for homogeneity check and validation of GA parameters. 
Take about 50 samples for PT and label them. The rest of the samples shall be used as CRMs, and they too should be labelled.
Packaging
The development of Certified Reference Materials (CRMs) follows a systematic process analogous to that employed in the preparation and conduct of proficiency testing (PT) schemes. The assignment of certified reference values is derived from statistically evaluated consensus means obtained through PT data analysis and validated measurement results.
Packaging of CRMs shall only commence once validation of all analytical parameters has been successfully completed and approved. Reference materials are dispensed from the homogenized bulk (homogeneity drum) into sealed, tamper-evident containers under controlled conditions to prevent contamination or material alteration.
 Each CRM unit shall be individually labelled immediately during packaging in accordance with the requirements of ISO 17034, clause 7.14 (this includes the 10 samples taken at random for testing), ensuring unique identification, traceability to the CRM batch, and clear reference to the relevant property values, intended use, and storage conditions.
Packaging shall not proceed, nor shall CRM units be released for storage, unless CRM identification labels are available and applied. In the event that permanent CRM labels are unavailable at the time of packaging, packaging activities shall be placed on hold, or the CRM units shall be clearly identified using an approved temporary identification system and quarantined from routine storage until permanent labels are applied. The use of temporary identification and subsequent labelling shall be documented and verified prior to release.
Only fully labelled, sealed, and verified CRM units are authorized for placement in the designated CRM storage facility. A verification step shall be performed to confirm correct labelling and batch allocation prior to storage and prior to any removal of CRM units for homogeneity testing or stability monitoring.
The designated CRM storage area shall be temperature-controlled between 16 °C and 20 °C and equipped with humidity monitoring devices. Environmental conditions shall be recorded twice weekly (every Monday and Friday), with visual checks performed throughout the week to ensure continued compliance with defined limits. All temperature and humidity monitoring records shall be documented, retained, and reviewed as part of the quality assurance system.Top of FormBottom of Form

4.2. For Hardgrove and Abrasive index (Eskom Method).
A minimum mass of 300 kg is required:
Air dry the entire sample, until visibly dry.
Cone and quarter the entire sample, take two opposites for PT and then cone and quarter the other two opposites. From this take two opposites and add to the first two PT opposites. Approximately 225 kg and 75 kg should be obtained for AI and HGI, respectively.
Prepare each of the two parameters separately from this point onwards.
Crush the sample at 25mm, 10mm, and 4.75mm in that order (i.e. stage crush).
Homogenise the sample for 2 hours in a mixing-drum. (Generates a lot of fines which is not ideal for HGI and AI analysis). Use cone-and-quartering to reduce the mass of the sample and transfer into the 210L drum ensuring that sample does not cover more than 75% of the area of the drum. Homogenise for 30 minutes.
Use the -1.0 µm sieve to screen the entire sample (removal of fines). Take the oversize and transfer into a container.
For HGI: Weigh 1.0 kg and transfer into a 1L bucket for packaging. Take all the material and keep it in the storage room. Some of the samples will be sent out for packaging while the rest will be stored as CRM.
For AI: Weigh ±9.5 kg and transfer into a 10L bucket for packaging. Send all the samples to the storage room. The required number will be distributed to participants while the rest is kept as CRM.
