1. Purpose
To define the systematic approach used by the laboratory to monitor the validity of test results and ensure continuing accuracy, reliability, and traceability in compliance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017, Clause 7.7. 
2. Scope
This procedure applies to all analytical preparation and determinations performed by the laboratory, including Preparation & Proximate Analysis (Moisture, Ash, Volatile Matter, and Fixed Carbon) and Calorific Value (CV) testing, as well as to all instruments and reference materials associated with these determinations.

4. Definitions
CRM: Certified Reference Material — a material with a certified analyte value and associated uncertainty.
Control Chart: Graphical tool used to monitor performance trends and detect bias or drift.
Repeatability Limit: Maximum allowable difference between duplicate results under the same conditions.
Verification: Confirmation that an instrument performs within defined limits before use.
5. Monitoring Methods
5.1 Instrument Calibration and Verification
Instruments that require calibration — Ash Furnace, Volatile Furnace, Moisture Oven, Drying Oven, Analytical Balances, and Mass Pieces — are sent to SANAS-accredited calibration facilities to ensure traceability to national standards.
Calibration certificates are reviewed upon receipt and retained in the instrument calibration file.
Verification prior to use:
Analysts perform daily or batch verification checks (e.g., temperature control and recording, balance verification, equipment efficiency) before starting work. 
Any deviations beyond control limits are reported to the Laboratory Manager/supervisor before testing proceeds.
CV instrument (Calorimeter) is calibrated in-house using a Certified Benzoic Acid pill of known calorific value. Calibration results are documented and plotted on a control chart to assess stability.

Results exceeding limits must be re-run, if it still is out of repeatability limit, run a CRM. If problem persists, do a quick verification/maintenance on instrument. Conduct an investigation if there is any drift found on verification/maintenance.
Certified Reference Material (CRM):
A CRM is analysed after every 10 routine runs for each parameter.
The measured value is compared with the certified value; deviations beyond control limits (reproducibility) are investigated.
Control Charts:
Control charts are maintained for each parameter using CRM and duplicate data.
Each new result is plotted and reviewed to detect bias, drift, or loss of precision.
Out-of-control points (beyond Reproducibility limits) or systematic trends require immediate suspension of affected runs until resolution.
5.3 Method Validation and Ongoing Verification
All methods are validated before routine use to confirm accuracy, precision, linearity, and detection limits.
Periodic re-validation is performed after any significant change (New equipment).
Analysts verify method performance using QC data trends and inter-analyst comparisons.
5.4 Proficiency Testing and Interlaboratory Comparison
The laboratory participates in proficiency testing (PT) for each accredited parameter.
PT performance is evaluated using z-scores and included in management review.
If no PT is available, inter-laboratory comparisons are conducted
5.5 Trending and Review
All QC data (duplicates, CRMs, calibrations, PT results) are trended per control chart.
The Laboratory Manager and Laboratory Supervisor jointly review control charts.
Any observed bias, drift, or degradation of performance triggers a formal Investigation.
Persistent trends may result in method re-validation, recalibration, or staff retraining.
6. Corrective Action Procedure
Stop analysis of samples for affected parameter or batch.
Re-verify instrument verification, calibrate where necessary and perform impact analysis on affected batches.
Document root cause, corrective and preventive action in the Nonconformance Register.
Confirm restored control with CRM or duplicate results before resuming routine testing.
